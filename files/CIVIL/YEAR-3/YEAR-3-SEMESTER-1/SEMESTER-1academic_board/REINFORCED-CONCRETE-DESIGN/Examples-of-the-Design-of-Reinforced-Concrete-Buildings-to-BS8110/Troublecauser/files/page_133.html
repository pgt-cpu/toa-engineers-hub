If movement of the wall and lateral strain are both prevented, the resulting earth pressure p corresponds to the so-called ‘at rest’ condition where p=kwh, in which k depends on the type of soil and other factors and w is the unit weight. For normally consolidated soils k is often taken as 1−sin θ, but it may range from 0.45 for loose sand to 6 for a clay that is machine compacted over the entire backfill. In the present case, with a soil having an angle of internal friction of 35°, 1−sin θ=0.426 and the intensity of pressure at a depth h is 0.426×16h= 6.82/h kN/m2. With a depth of wall of 3.45 m, the pressure at the bottom is now 6.82×3.45=23.53 kN/m2, decreasing uniformly as before, and the additional pressure due to surcharge becomes 0.426×10= 4.26 kN/m2.
